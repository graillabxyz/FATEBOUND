# Comparative design research

OMNIPATH borrows design questions, not Card text or Magic's colors. Cards are guaranteed, reusable tools in a four-Card Hand, so a one-use spell's efficiency cannot be copied directly.

Primary comparative references were Scryfall records for [Alpha Counterspell](https://scryfall.com/card/lea/54/counterspell), [Beta Lightning Bolt](https://scryfall.com/card/leb/162/lightning-bolt), [Unlimited Swords to Plowshares](https://scryfall.com/card/2ed/41/swords-to-plowshares), [Arabian Nights Juzám Djinn](https://scryfall.com/card/arn/29/juz%C3%A1m-djinn), [Antiquities Candelabra of Tawnos](https://scryfall.com/card/atq/43/candelabra-of-tawnos), [Legends Moat](https://scryfall.com/card/leg/28/moat), and [The Dark Ashes to Ashes](https://scryfall.com/card/drk/39/ashes-to-ashes). These span response timing, immediate efficiency, drawbacks, ongoing opportunity costs, conversion engines and broad denial.

The resulting OMNIPATH principles are:

- Guaranteed reusable cancellation needs an exact Omen requirement and a real Focus cost. It must not generate an infinite response chain.
- Efficient offense should compete with holding a useful Reaction, rather than converting every higher Value to more damage.
- Life costs are paid before the opponent responds; canceling the effect does not return its cost.
- Resource conversion must spend actual Omens and must not create repeatable free loops.
- Avoid permanent broad locks in this alpha. Every current Card resolves without creating a battlefield permanent.
- Rarity measures specialization and complexity, not a numerical power multiplier. Commons remain efficient, readable tools.

Wizards' [Common Courtesy](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/making-magic/common-courtesy-2002-06-10) and [Quite a Rarity](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/making-magic/quite-rarity-2018-03-12) inform the separation of baseline accessibility, complexity and rarity. OMNIPATH's utility weights are diagnostic estimates, not proof of competitive strength.
