Build the initial production-quality foundation for a MOBILE-ONLY competitive freemium strategy game based on folklore, Legends, reusable cards, customizable dice, shared Fate, hidden information, prediction, bluffing, and deterministic tactical decision-making.

The game should feel like a very small TCG in terms of collecting, customization, matchups, mastery, ranked play, cosmetics, and identity, but it must be dramatically easier to understand than a traditional TCG.

The core philosophy is:

RNG should create the puzzle.

RNG should NOT decide who wins.

Intelligence, prediction, build construction, tactical planning, resource management, opponent knowledge, bluffing, and adaptation should determine the winner.

The game should be designed from the beginning as a portrait-oriented mobile game for iOS and Android.

Do not design a desktop-first interface and shrink it later.

The entire UX should feel native to a phone.

Use large touch targets, thumb-friendly controls, strong visual hierarchy, fast transitions, short matches, minimal text, and readable cards.

The initial target is approximately 3–5 minute competitive matches.

====================

There are NO traditional decks.

There is NO draw pile.

There is NO discard pile.

There is NO opening hand RNG.

There is NO random card draw.

Each player builds a fixed combat loadout consisting of:

4 reusable Cards
3 Dice

The Legend is always public.

The 4 cards are private at the start of the match.

5. SHARED FATE SYSTEM
=====================

This system is critical.

Do NOT allow one player to simply roll better than the other.

Instead, both players receive the SAME underlying random Fate positions.

Example:

Fate generates:

Face Position 2
Face Position 5
Face Position 3

Player A's first equipped die lands on its own face 2.
Player B's first equipped die lands on its own face 2.

Player A's second die lands on its own face 5.
Player B's second die lands on its own face 5.

And so on.

Therefore both players are exposed to the same random event.

Their different equipped dice interpret that Fate differently.

This is the central competitive fairness principle.

Randomness determines the situation.

The player's build and decisions determine the result.

Use a server-authoritative or otherwise deterministic match seed architecture so both clients can reproduce the same Fate state reliably.

The game should eventually support replay verification.

==================================================
6. CONTROL RESOURCE
===================

Each round, each player receives a small amount of CONTROL.

Prototype with:

2 Control per round.

Control resets every round.

Control does not bank unless a special future card explicitly allows it.

Control should allow deterministic dice manipulation.

Initial universal actions:

1 Control:
SHIFT a numbered die one step higher or lower where valid.

2 Control:
FLIP a die to its designed opposite face.

Each die definition should know which face is opposite which other face.

For example on a d6:

1 ↔ 6
2 ↔ 5
3 ↔ 4

Custom dice follow the same positional principle.

If face 1 is Blank and face 6 is Strike, flipping Blank creates Strike.

This makes bad-looking faces tactically meaningful because they may have strong opposites.

Avoid making reroll the default manipulation mechanic.

Rerolls reintroduce gambling.

Manipulation should primarily be deterministic.

Future abilities may allow rerolls as special mechanics.

7. ROUND STRUCTURE
==================

Build the battle loop around this sequence:

PHASE 1:
FATE

Generate three shared Fate face positions.

Animate Fate visually.

PHASE 2:
ROLL

Both players' equipped dice animate and resolve to the relevant faces.

The same Fate positions apply to both players.

PHASE 3:
CONTROL

Players inspect their dice and optionally spend Control to manipulate them.

PHASE 4:
ASSIGN

Players secretly assign their dice to actions.

A die may be assigned to:

A card
Legend ability
Universal Guard
Potential future contextual action

Players may use fewer than all three dice.

Some cards may require two dice.

PHASE 5:
LOCK

Player confirms their assignments.

Show a clear locked state.

PHASE 6:
REVEAL

Both players' selected cards and assignments are revealed simultaneously.

PHASE 7:
RESOLVE

Resolve effects according to a deterministic priority system.

PHASE 8:
CLEANUP

Apply status expiry.
Return reusable cards to hand.
Update known-card state.
Reset Control.
Advance round.

Then begin next Fate.
